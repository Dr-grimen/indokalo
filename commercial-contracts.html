A useful introduction needs more than a name.

  1. A clear brief. What is needed, who makes the decision, and which requirements matter most.
  2. A focused search. Potential companies checked against the brief, including location, capabilities and interest.
  3. A clear picture of the fit. Confirmed details, open questions and any gaps that need resolving before a quote.
  4. An agreed introduction. Sharing and fee terms settled first. Buyer and supplier then discuss, negotiate and sign their own contract.

You will know whether a company is simply a research candidate or has confirmed its interest. An introduction gives you a conversation to explore; it does not guarantee an order.

No upfront fee. Clear terms first.

Buyers don’t pay me for an introduction. Supplier commission is payable only if a deal results under the written terms agreed before the introduction. Those terms set out the calculation, payment trigger and due date.
